# Consensus voters outside the bounded record neighbourhood: a leader that can never regain a majority (§4.8, D-14)

- **Date:** 2026-09-13
- **Subsystem:** the fleet's per-peer record sessions and the consensus drives that ride them (`crates/server/src/fleet.rs`); the configuration council and the root group.
- **Severity:** liveness — a root or council leader whose in-copyset voters die can never replicate to the surviving voters, so nothing commits again; the survivors campaign forever. Not a safety breach: nothing incorrect is committed, no split-brain.
- **Found by:** `crates/server/tests/fleet.rs::a_root_learner_fetches_the_committed_region_membership_over_the_transport` under 12 busy-spin processes on the 18-core shared box (task #32, "robust to noisy and heavy CPU load"). The test passes at rest and, before this fix, passed under load only when the election happened to be won by a node whose copyset contained the surviving voter.

## Symptom

After the victim (a single-host region's representative, a root voter) is killed and its death injected into every survivor, the surviving root leader must commit the lost region's retirement and the learner must fetch it. Under load the test ran to its cap. Instrumentation showed a **stable** leader (1516 of 1529 coordinator periods in one run) that **never ran a replication round**: every period its `take_sessions` over the other root voters returned nothing, its committed root configuration stayed at `version 0, regions 3`, its live follower's leader-contact stayed at 0 (it never received an append) while that follower aged out and campaigned every ~18 periods (its pre-votes refused by the live leader), and the learner saw region divergence every period (`wanted=true` ×1508) with nothing to fetch.

## Investigation — four hypotheses measured before the cause

Each was instrumented and measured on the same test under the same load; each is recorded so it is not re-tried.

1. **Election timeout too short for the broadcast RTT** (the design's "election timeout ≥ 10 × broadcast RTT p99"). Measured: SWIM probe RTT p99 17 ms, max 21 ms (4555 samples); consensus broadcast round-trip p50 11 ms, p99 33 ms. Both far inside one 100 ms heartbeat, so a period-counted timeout derived from either sits at its floor of 10 — **inert on one host**, cannot be the cause. It stays owed for a real WAN; the fixed 10× heartbeat ratio remains (`ELECTION_HEARTBEATS`).
2. **Follower timer not reset on granting a vote** (Raft Figure 2). Real deviation, fixed as a sibling (below); no change to the campaign rate, because 32 of 33 pre-vote rounds *failed* (one voter dead, the other the refusing leader) and the real-vote path was rarely reached.
3. **Follower timer not reset on a current-leader append the log check rejects** (Raft Figure 2). Real deviation, fixed as a sibling; a counter showed the case fired **zero** times — the follower received no appends at all.
4. **A voter's session orphaned by `broadcast`.** `broadcast`'s progress-aware stop (the 2026-09-12 dead-voter fix, `docs/bugs/2026-09-12-broadcast-waits-out-dead-voter.md`) detached late children and dropped its reply channel, so a late voter's endpoint was lost while its `record_sessions` slot stayed `Some(None)` — which the per-peer link task never re-dials (it re-establishes only an *absent* key) — and the `broadcast` comment claiming "the link task re-establishes it" was false. A real defect, fixed as a sibling (below). But it was **not this bug's cause**: the leader's view was `others=2 absent=1 none=1`, and the `None` slot was misattributed to the live follower without checking which host was which. Two fixes for it — forget-and-redial, then the completed straggler port — each passed the test once in ~5 s and then capped 4 of 5 runs at 300 s at the same baseline load. The forget-and-redial variant is also recorded as measured-and-rejected: it re-dialed a session every round while still dropping the acknowledgement.

Node-tagged tracing of every `take_sessions`/`return_sessions`/dispatch on the leader then attributed the slots. The `None` slot was the **dead victim's**, borrowed every period by the *council* (its append to the dead voter timing out and returning late as a straggler). The `absent` slot was the **live follower's — the leader had never established a session to it at all** (no link-establish for that pair, in any run), while the follower *had* one to the leader (its pre-votes reached it). Every node had established sessions to exactly two of its three peers.

## Root cause

The link tasks (`establish_record_link`, one per roster peer) keep a record session only to peers in this owner's **record neighbourhood** — the copyset `select_neighbourhood` bounds to the scatter width, at the candidate floor `2f + 1` the owner plus `2f` holders (`crates/db/src/register.rs::select_neighbourhood`, D-14) — and idle any other peer as "retired". That bound is right for the record plane. But the **configuration council and the root group ride the same per-peer record session** (their streams multiplex on it, `take_sessions` over their voters), and their voter sets are *not* subsets of the copyset: a root voter is another region's representative; a council voter need not be a candidate holder. A consensus voter outside a node's copyset is therefore unreachable from that node, permanently — by construction, at rest, not only under load.

Why it hid: an election needs only a majority. With three root voters the leader-to-be reached the one voter in its copyset, won with self + 1, and committed the same way — never touching the third voter. Load merely made the test's kill land while that arrangement held; killing the one in-copyset voter left a leader whose only remaining voter it could never reach, and no re-election could help (the survivor's pre-votes were correctly refused by a live leader). The earlier "orphaned session" reading was the council's dead-voter slot, a coincidence of the same kill.

A measurement artefact prolonged the misdiagnosis and is recorded so it is not repeated: a probe placed *after* the link task's `continue` for retired peers logged `retired=false` for 100% of iterations — because it never ran for the retired ones. Instrument before the early-continue, or count both branches.

## Fix

`crates/server/src/fleet.rs`: `keeps_record_session_to(state, peer)` — the set of peers this node keeps a record session to is everything `take_sessions` is ever asked for: the record neighbourhood **or a voter of this region's council or of the root group**. `establish_record_link`'s retire predicate uses it. A learner reaches voters to fetch; a voter reaches voters to replicate and elect. Both voter sets are small and bounded (one representative per region; an elected council), and a dead voter's link idles the moment its group commits its retirement — which reaching the surviving voters is exactly what makes possible.

## Sibling fixes shipped in the same change (each a real defect, none this bug's cause)

- **`broadcast` no longer drops its stragglers.** It was an incomplete port of the record plane's fan-out: `commit_record` returns at its progress-aware stop *and* hands its open reply channel back as `Stragglers::pending(rx)`, which the coordinator settles through `Dispatch::settle`. `broadcast` now does the same, and every consensus and learner-fetch round is an ordinary `Dispatch` on the coordinator's `in_flight`. `Dispatch` carries a `LateReplies` policy: the record plane says `Discard` (it re-ships idempotently); a council or root round says `Council`/`Root` and **folds** a late `AppendReply`/`VoteReply` when it arrives (`Stragglers::recover_replies`) — standard Raft, whose leader rules (Ongaro & Ousterhout 2014, Figure 2) process every response on arrival gated only by term, and whose `on_append_reply`/`on_vote_reply` here already ignore a stale-term or wrong-role reply; a late pre-vote reply is not folded (it would owe follow-on vote requests a settle cannot ship). A learner fetch says `ConfigFetch`/`RootFetch` and adopts a late newer configuration. `slates_cluster::{Reply, Stragglers::pending, Stragglers::none}` are public for it.
- **Raft Figure 2 follower timer-resets** (`crates/cluster/src/config_group.rs`, `root_group.rs`, `answer`): a **granted vote** advances `leader_contact`; a **current-or-newer-term append** advances it **even when the log-consistency check rejects it** (`append_term >= reply.term`), replacing the `reply.success`-only gate. Four by-use unit tests.
- **The daemon's test-facing API no longer swallows `ControlFull`** (`crates/server/src/daemon.rs`). `observe` and `observe_peer_dead` spawn a task onto the control shard through its bounded control channel, which under load is routinely full; a refused spawn returned `None`/nothing at once — the harness's own false negative under exactly the load it exists to prove robustness against (an injected death that never landed; a leader that "was not"; a region membership "empty", which the `learned` predicate would read as *learned*). `spawn_admitted` retries until admitted or the budget elapses, parking a tenth of a period between attempts — the tree's policy for a full control channel is backpressure, never a drop (`verbs::forward`; `fleet::fan_configs_to_shards`). `observe_peer_dead` now returns whether the death landed and folded within the observe budget, and the test asserts it.

## Sibling findings (reported, not fixed here)

- `Raft::check_quorum` (CheckQuorum, §6.2) is implemented and unit-tested but never driven by the coordinator, so a leader cut off from its followers never steps down.
- The observation accessors (`root_regions`, `root_leads`, …) map a *genuine* observe timeout to a default value (empty / `false`); a predicate like `!root_regions().contains(&lost)` is then true on a wedged shard. The honest type is `Option<T>`, with the poll treating `None` as "not yet"; owed.
- SWIM probes follow the same record neighbourhood, so a node learns a consensus voter's liveness outside its copyset only by gossip; direct probing of consensus voters would tighten leader liveness detection.

## Validation

Same test, same 12 busy-spin processes, on the fixed tree (2026-09-13 11:18, box baseline load 10.67 before the spinners — *higher* than the failing runs' ~5): **passes in 5.37 s**. The phase trace: fleet formed 3.6 s, root leader elected 4.9 s, victim killed and death injected 4.9 s, **learner learned 5.2 s** — the leader's first replication round after the kill reached both root voters (`sent=2`), the survivor acknowledged, the retirement committed, the learner fetched it. Pre-fix under comparable load: 0 replication rounds in 1516 leader periods and a cap.

**Repeat on the final tree** (2026-09-13 11:20, baseline 5.46 before the spinners — the same baseline at which the previous trees capped 0 of 3 runs at 300 s): **3 of 3 pass, 6.16 s, 5.75 s, 5.83 s.**

One caveat for anyone re-running this on the shared box: judge a load run by its baseline before the spinners, not only by the spinner count — an earlier run's `uptime` showed a 1-minute load of 21.4 at the instant the spinners started (an exponentially-damped average cannot jump that far in seconds), i.e. other users already had the machine at ~20, and under that much external oversubscription no period-driven convergence is expected inside a fixed cap.
