http, git: red tests for HTTP/1.1's shared request codec and git's smart HTTP routes, the codec moved into its own crate (A-595, §4.17.2 piece 8's first part)

The request codec leaves slates-git for a crate of its own, slates-http, so the Mercurial and Subversion servers share it
without depending on git's crate; slates-git keeps git's routes and replies. Each body is a stub here; the tests are the
specification:
- head: a resumable reader borrowing the caller's bytes; every cut partial with nothing charged; the head's bytes and
  field index charged exactly; 414 and 431 past the room or MAX_LINE, whole or still open; the grammar's every break
  named (bare CR or LF, obs-fold, whitespace before a colon, controls, targets in no form); Host once in HTTP/1.1;
  Content-Length digits that agree; every framing two readers could disagree on refused 400 or 501; gzip alone 415;
  Expect 417; refusals judged in one order whatever the fields' order; statuses and closing replies.
- body: Content-Length and chunked streamed as slices of the input, every cut and a byte at a time; extensions skipped,
  trailers dropped and charged; hostile chunk lines and trailers refused by kind; a proptest against a reference
  chunked encoder over any cuts.
- gzip: inflated and charged exactly, every cut; every truncation refused at the finish; a 16 MiB bomb stopped at a
  1 MiB room; foreign magic, bad CRC and length, trailing garbage; two members; a full header; fed after its end.
- percent, reply: escapes, forms, escaped slashes; heads byte for byte, response splitting refused, chunks.
- git: routes as http-backend routes them (dumb paths 403, upload-archive 403, 405 Allow per route and 400 to
  HTTP/1.0, 404 for alias paths, 415, 411, decoded paths and forms, the last service winning), Git-Protocol's
  greatest version as protocol.c chooses it (the earlier codec took the last), the prelude, and replies with
  http-backend's cache fields in its order.
